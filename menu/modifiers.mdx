Modifiers let your staff capture exactly how a guest wants their order — from steak cook level to add-on toppings — right at the point of sale. Well-configured modifiers reduce miscommunication between the floor and the kitchen, ensure accurate tickets, and create natural upsell opportunities for items like premium add-ons or size upgrades. Every modifier selection is printed on the kitchen ticket so your team never has to guess.

## What Is a Modifier Group?

A modifier group is a named set of options that you attach to one or more menu items. When a server adds that item to an order, Gratly prompts them to make a selection from each attached group.

Here are common examples:

- **Cook Level** → Rare, Medium Rare, Medium, Well Done *(required, pick 1)*
- **Add-ons** → Extra Cheese, Bacon, Avocado, Fried Egg *(optional, pick multiple)*
- **Drink Size** → Small, Medium, Large *(required, pick 1)*

Each group has two key settings:

- **Selection type** — *Single* (the server picks exactly one option) or *Multiple* (the server can pick any number of options).
- **Required or Optional** — *Required* groups must be answered before the item can be added to the order. *Optional* groups can be skipped.

## Creating a Modifier Group

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Modifiers">
    From the Gratly dashboard, go to **Menu > Modifiers**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start a new group">
    Tap **+ New Group**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the group">
    Enter a descriptive name that your staff will recognize on the order screen, such as **Cook Level**, **Add-ons**, or **Drink Size**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the selection type">
    Choose **Single** if the server should pick exactly one option, or **Multiple** if they can select several.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark as Required or Optional">
    Toggle **Required** on if this group must be answered before the item can be ordered. Leave it off for optional upsells or preferences.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add options">
    Tap **+ Add Option** for each choice. Enter the option name (e.g., *Medium Rare*) and, if applicable, a price adjustment (e.g., *+$1.50* or *-$0.50*). Leave the price adjustment blank for options that don't affect the total.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Tap **Save**. The modifier group is now available to attach to any menu item.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Attaching Modifiers to Items

Once a modifier group exists, attach it to any item that needs it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the item">
    Go to **Menu > Categories**, select the relevant category, and tap the item name to open its detail screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate to Modifier Groups">
    Scroll down to the **Modifier Groups** section on the item detail screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach a group">
    Tap **Attach Group**. A list of all your existing modifier groups appears.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the group">
    Tap the modifier group you want to attach. You can attach multiple groups to a single item — they will be presented to the server in the order they appear on the item screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Tap **Save**. The modifier group is now active for that item and will appear during order entry on every terminal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Modifier Pricing

Each option within a modifier group can carry a price adjustment that is added to or subtracted from the item's base price at the time of ordering.

For example, if a **House Burger** costs $14.00 and a guest selects **Add Avocado (+$1.50)**, the item rings up at $15.50. If an option like **No Bun** carries a credit of **-$0.50**, the item rings up at $13.50 instead.

Price adjustments appear as line details on the POS screen, the kitchen ticket, and the guest's receipt so everything is transparent end to end.

<Tip>
  Modifier groups are shared and reusable. If you attach the **Cook Level** group to your burger, steak, and lamb chop items, any change you make to that group — adding a new option, adjusting a price — updates all three items at once. Build groups with reuse in mind to save time as your menu grows.
</Tip>
